KEY FINDINGS — Employee Workforce Dashboard
============================================
(Data note: Age, Salary and Rating are 27-42% missing in this dataset, so all
figures below use only the records that have data and are directional, not final.)

1. Gender pay gap: Men earn ~22% more than women on average (Rs 80.8k vs Rs 66.1k),
   with almost identical performance ratings (3.46 vs 3.45). Worth an HR pay-equity
   review.

2. Weak pay-performance link: Salary and Rating have only a weak positive
   relationship (Spearman correlation 0.25). Several employees earning above
   Rs 90k are rated 2 or below, and several earning under Rs 45k are rated
   4.5 or higher.

3. Pay compression by tenure: Newer hires are paid more but rated lower. Average
   salary rises steadily by join year (Rs 65k in 2019 to Rs 85k in 2022), while
   average rating falls from 4.1 to about 3.3 over the same period.

4. Department and city differences: HR has the highest average salary (Rs 83.7k)
   and Sales the lowest (Rs 66.4k). IT has the top rating (4.8) and Finance the
   lowest (2.9). Delhi and Mumbai pay the most; Ahmedabad pays the least.

5. Attrition signal: 55% of employees with a known status are inactive. Inactivity
   is highest in HR (75%) and Sales (71%), and lowest in Finance (33%). Inactive
   employees are younger on average (34.7 vs 40.5 years), suggesting the company
   may be losing younger staff.

RECOMMENDATIONS
================
1. Fix the source-data gaps (Age, Salary, Rating, Join_Date) before deeper modelling.
2. Audit pay by gender and by join-year cohort.
3. Investigate why HR and Sales have unusually high inactivity.
4. Introduce performance-linked pay bands, since pay currently tracks tenure more
   than performance.
